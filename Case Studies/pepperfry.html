Turning customer insight into a clearer shopping experience

At Pepperfry, I led the pre-cart shopping journey across web and mobile during a major platform redesign—and built a dedicated discovery practice to help the team connect customer evidence with product and engineering decisions.

01 / Context

A platform rebuild—and a customer journey to improve.

Pepperfry was modernizing its digital platform, moving from a legacy PHP environment toward Node.js and Angular while working toward a more consistent design system across web and mobile.

I joined as Brand Manager for Unbevel, the in-house furniture brand, where I managed sourcing, supplier negotiation, assortment, pricing, sales and P&L. I later moved into Product to lead the pre-cart shopping experience through the redesign. That earlier commercial role gave me a useful view of the relationship between customer experience, merchandising and business performance.

02 / The mandate

Make discovery a repeatable input to product decisions.

I owned roadmap priorities, backlog, requirements, user stories and sprint planning for the shopping experience, working with roughly 20 engineers across three platforms. The remit was not simply to ship redesigned screens: it was to identify where customers were getting stuck and help Product, Business and IT make decisions from a shared evidence base.

There was no dedicated discovery capability in place for this work. I recruited research and design support and introduced a structured, recurring practice for user interviews, with findings feeding directly into roadmap discussions.

The central challenge: turn customer signals into focused improvements while the underlying technology and design system were changing at the same time.

03 / Decisions

Three moves connected insight to delivery.

Discovery helped make the problem more specific. Product ownership then meant translating that evidence into a sequence of decisions the wider team could execute.

01

Build discovery into the process

Established recurring user interviews and brought research findings into roadmap decisions, instead of relying only on stakeholder assumptions or ad hoc feedback.

02

Investigate the friction points

Customer-journey analytics highlighted search and search results as a major drop-off point. Interviews also exposed confusion about product price, MRP, discounts and cart-level pricing.

03

Coordinate changes across teams

Worked with Checkout on clearer price presentation and used A/B testing to validate the change. Balanced customer-facing priorities with the constraints and sequencing of the platform migration.

The product loop
LISTEN & OBSERVE

Understand the journey

Recurring customer interviews, analytics and search behavior to locate uncertainty and drop-off.

PRIORITIZE & TEST

Make the problem actionable

Roadmap choices, hypotheses and A/B testing—partnering with Checkout when the issue crossed product boundaries.

DELIVER & LEARN

Ship within real constraints

Requirements, user stories and sprint planning coordinated across web, iOS, Android and a changing technical platform.

04 / Impact

Stronger evidence. Clearer priorities. Coordinated delivery.

The redesign shipped across web, iOS and Android. Conversion was reported at +12% year over year during this period. The work also established a more structured discovery practice, giving the team a repeatable way to bring customer evidence into future roadmap decisions.

Search and pricing emerged as important areas of customer friction, and the pricing work was tested with the Checkout team through A/B testing. In parallel, product priorities were sequenced alongside the broader transition from legacy PHP toward Node.js and Angular and the evolution of the design system.

05 / Reflection

Build discovery early enough to shape the brief.

Looking back, I would establish the discovery function before the redesign formally kicked off. Building it alongside an active transformation created momentum, but earlier research could have helped shape scope and surface customer questions before delivery decisions were already in motion.

What I took forward: good product leadership connects customer evidence, commercial understanding and engineering reality early—then turns that shared picture into clear priorities and testable decisions.
