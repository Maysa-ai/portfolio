01 / Context

A product with several needs to bring together.

Physio.me connected patients with physiotherapists through guided exercise. Physiotherapists could manage and share exercises; patients could follow their programs and report on their experience.

I joined as a consultant to take an existing healthcare MVP into live operation and continue product development. The work sat at the intersection of patient experience, professional workflows, medical input, business priorities, and regulatory requirements.

Patient experience

Make onboarding and the ongoing exercise journey clear and usable for people receiving care.

Professional workflow

Support physiotherapists in managing patients, exercises, and the service around them.

Trust by design

Account for privacy, security, and professional identity verification as product requirements.

02 / Leadership

Three decisions that shaped the work.

My role was to create product direction across competing needs, turn discovery into a prioritized roadmap, and keep the team moving within real delivery and compliance constraints.

01

Give stakeholders a shared way to prioritize

Introduced a clearer North Star and prioritization framework to bring patients, physiotherapists, medical stakeholders, business teams, Legal, and Engineering around a more consistent logic for roadmap decisions—rather than treating every request as a separate negotiation.

02

Build trust and verification into the product

Worked across onboarding, authentication, exercise management, and physiotherapist identity-verification requirements involving official French professional databases, including Adeli/Via-related processes. GDPR, privacy, and security considerations shaped product requirements alongside the technical manager and Legal.

03

Connect research, delivery, and rollout preparation

Organized cross-functional discovery with medical and patient-facing stakeholders, then coordinated a structured post-launch research program with an external team. I translated findings into prioritized improvements while managing development budget, delivery coordination, localization, translation, and content preparation for additional markets.

03 / Product loop

From stakeholder input to product action.

Discovery and research were not separate from delivery. They helped clarify what mattered, what needed to change, and what the platform needed before it could be prepared for other markets.

Leadership takeaway
In healthcare, product alignment means making patient needs, professional trust, and regulatory requirements part of the same product decision.

This is the kind of product leadership I bring: creating a shared direction, making trade-offs explicit, and connecting evidence to delivery without losing sight of the people who rely on the product.
